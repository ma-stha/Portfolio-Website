Why most GA4 setups lie to you (and five fixes)

Your dashboard says sales are up. Your bank account disagrees. Here are the five GA4 settings behind most of the gap, and how to fix each in under an hour.

Manjil ShresthaDigital marketer · Melbourne
14 October 20267 min read

    On this page

      TL;DR

      • In the audits I run, most GA4 properties miscount conversions somewhere between "a bit" and "wildly".
      • Five settings cause most of it: duplicate purchases, the wrong key events, internal traffic, payment-gateway referrals and short data retention.
      • All five are quick to fix. There's a checklist at the end.

      Every week someone shows me a GA4 report that looks great and a revenue number that doesn't. Nobody lied on purpose. GA4 just does exactly what it's told, and most properties were told the wrong things during a rushed setup two years ago.

      00The quiet problem

      Bad tracking rarely breaks loudly. It drifts. A thank-you page that fires twice, a payment provider that steals the credit for a sale, your own team browsing the site all day. Each one nudges the numbers a little, and every budget decision you make sits on top of them. true story: one client's "record month" was a thank-you page firing twice.

      01Fix duplicate purchases

      If a customer reloads the order confirmation page, or comes back to it from an email, many setups send a second purchase event. Your revenue in GA4 creeps above your real revenue, and your ROAS looks better than it is.

      The fix is to always send a unique transaction_id with every purchase. GA4 uses it to recognise repeat purchase events for the same order.

      // Push this once, on the order confirmation page
dataLayer.push({ ecommerce: null });
dataLayer.push({
  event: "purchase",
  ecommerce: {
    transaction_id: "ORD-10482",   // unique per order
    value: 189.00,
    currency: "AUD"
  }
});
      ✦

      Quick test: in GA4, compare purchases per week with orders from Shopify or your CRM. If GA4 is consistently higher, duplicates are the first suspect.

      02Mark the right key events

      In 2024 Google renamed GA4 "conversions" to key events. Whatever you call them, they should be the handful of actions that mean money: a purchase, a qualified lead, a booked call. I regularly find properties where page views or scrolls are marked as key events, which makes every channel look like a winner.

      EventKey event?Why
      purchaseYesDirect revenue
      generate_leadYesA real enquiry, once spam is filtered
      book_callYesStrongest B2B intent signal
      scrollNoEngagement, not an outcome
      page_viewNoInflates every report it touches

      03Filter out your own traffic

      Your team, your developers and your agency visit the site constantly. In a small business that can be a noticeable share of sessions and test conversions.

      1. In Admin, open your web data stream and choose Configure tag settings, then Define internal traffic. Add your office and home IP addresses.
      2. In Admin, open Data filters. The internal traffic filter is created in a Testing state.
      3. Check it in reports for a few days, then switch it to Active.
      !

      Watch out: filters only apply from the moment they're active. They don't clean historical data, so do this early.

      04Stop payment gateways stealing credit

      When a customer pays through PayPal, Afterpay or a hosted checkout and returns to your site, GA4 can start a new session and credit the sale to that payment domain as a "referral". Your Google Ads campaign did the work and PayPal gets the trophy. PayPal has never once run your ads. stop thanking it.

      Open your web stream's tag settings and use List unwanted referrals to add the payment domains you use, for example paypal.com, afterpay.com or checkout.stripe.com.

      You can't optimise what you can't trust. Fix the measurement first, then argue about the strategy.— Manjil Shrestha

      05Keep 14 months of data, not 2

      GA4 standard properties default to keeping event-level data for 2 months. That limit applies to explorations, the custom reports you use for deeper analysis. Change it to 14 months under Admin, Data retention, so you can compare this season with last year's.

      It takes a minute and it isn't retroactive, so the sooner the better.
