How to name a business in Australia without regret

A great name is short, ownable and legally clear. Here is the naming process I use, plus the Australian checks to run before you print anything.

Contents

    7 min left

    Contents

      TL;DR

      • Generate widely, then filter hard: meaning, sound, length and spelling.
      • In Australia, check the ASIC business names register and IP Australia trade marks before you fall in love.
      • Registering a business name does not give you ownership of it. A trade mark does far more.

      Naming is the most emotional part of branding. Everyone has an opinion, every good name seems taken, and the one you love turns out to mean something rude in another language. Here's how to get through it calmly.

      01Start with territories, not names

      Before brainstorming, decide what kinds of names you're open to. I usually explore four territories:

      • Descriptive: says what you do. Clear, but hard to protect.
      • Evocative: suggests a feeling or story, like Saltbush or Little Creek.
      • Invented: a new word. Easy to own, harder to explain.
      • Founder or place: personal and credible, but less flexible if you grow.

      02Filter like a brand strategist

      Score your shortlist out of five on each of these, and be honest. Say it out loud on a phone call. If you have to spell it twice, it's a problem.

      TestQuestion
      MeaningDoes it support our positioning?
      SoundIs it easy to say, hear and remember?
      SpellingCan people type it after hearing it once?
      LengthDoes it fit on a cup, an app icon and a sign?
      OwnabilityCan we protect it and get a usable domain?

      03The Australian checks

      Run these before you share the name with anyone outside the team:

      1. ASIC business names register: is the exact name already registered?
      2. IP Australia trade mark search: is anything similar registered for related goods or services?
      3. Domains: a .com.au needs an Australian presence, such as an ABN. Check .com too.
      4. Socials: are the handles free on the platforms you'll actually use?
      !

      Watch out: a registered business name lets you trade under it. It doesn't stop others using something similar. For real protection, talk to a trade mark attorney or apply through IP Australia.

      04How to decide

      Put your top three on real things: a mock shopfront, a business card, an Instagram profile. Live with them for a week. The right name usually stops feeling strange and starts feeling inevitable.

      A good name doesn't have to explain everything. It has to be worth remembering.Manjil Shrestha
