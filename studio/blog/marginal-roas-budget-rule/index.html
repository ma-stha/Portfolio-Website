Marginal ROAS: the only budget rule you need

Average ROAS tells you how your ads did. Marginal ROAS tells you what your next dollar will do. Only one of those should decide your budget.

LinkedIn ↗X ↗

Contents

    5 min left

    Contents

      TL;DR

      • Average ROAS hides diminishing returns. The last dollars you spend usually return far less than the first.
      • Your breakeven ROAS is 1 ÷ gross margin. At a 40% margin, that's 2.5x.
      • Keep adding budget while marginal ROAS beats breakeven. Move money from the channel with the lowest marginal ROAS to the highest.

      "Our ROAS is 2.4x, so let's spend more." It sounds sensible, and it's how a lot of good businesses quietly lose money on ads. The problem is the word average.

      01Average vs marginal, in one table

      Monthly spendRevenueAverage ROASExtra revenue from last $5kMarginal ROAS
      $5,000$20,0004.0x$20,0004.0x
      $10,000$34,0003.4x$14,0002.8x
      $15,000$43,0002.9x$9,0001.8x
      $20,000$48,0002.4x$5,0001.0x

      At $20k a month, the average ROAS of 2.4x looks close to fine. But the last $5,000 brought in just $5,000. That's a marginal ROAS of 1.0x, which loses money for almost any business. the average is a polite liar.

      02Find your breakeven ROAS

      Breakeven ROAS = 1 ÷ gross margin. With a 40% gross margin, every dollar of ad spend needs $2.50 of revenue to pay for itself. In the table above, the third and fourth $5k blocks both fall below 2.5x.

      40%gross margin
      2.5xbreakeven ROAS
      $10kthe spend level to stop at

      03Estimate it without a data team

      • Step tests: change one campaign's budget by about 20% for two weeks and compare the extra revenue to the extra spend.
      • Platform planners: Google Ads' budget simulators and Performance Planner estimate the curve for you. Treat them as a starting point, not gospel.
      • Blended view: check total revenue against total spend (MER) so channels can't all claim the same sale.

      04The rule

      Spend the next dollar where it earns the most, and stop when it earns less than it costs.— The whole strategy, really

      Review it monthly. Curves shift with seasonality, creative fatigue and competition. If you want help building the model for your account, book a free call.
